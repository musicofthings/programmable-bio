Future expansion · Not operational

Hyderabad first. APAC nodes later.

BioHelix is a working name for a possible future Singapore or Southeast Asia node. Programmable Bio does not currently operate a Singapore lab and does not claim a signed MiRXES partnership.

Status: concept only. The operational asset today is the compact autonomous R&D lab in Hyderabad.
Expansion logic

Replicate a proven cell, not an untested blueprint

The right time to add another geography is after the Hyderabad cell has repeatable customer demand, stable methods, measurable utilization, and a documented quality system. A new node should copy a working operating unit while adding capabilities that the home site cannot provide efficiently.

Regional customer access

Shorter logistics and stronger commercial coverage for Southeast Asian academic, biotech, and pharma teams.

Regulatory and manufacturing ecosystem

Potential access to Singapore's translational research, diagnostics, and advanced-manufacturing networks.

Capacity resilience

A second qualified node can absorb demand and reduce reliance on one physical site.

Method portability

LabOS should make a validated method reproducible across compatible cells, instruments, and operators.

Decision gates

What must be true before expansion

1

Hyderabad unit economics

Demonstrated utilization, reproducibility, turnaround, demand, and gross-margin performance.

2

Customer pull

Named APAC customers or partners whose needs justify local execution rather than a prestige address.

3

Signed operating partner or owned-site plan

Defined responsibilities, quality system, IP terms, regulatory scope, and economics.

4

Portable methods

Evidence that LabOS protocols and data contracts transfer without degrading assay performance.

The current moat is execution. A Singapore entity or partnership becomes useful only when it makes the operating network better.
