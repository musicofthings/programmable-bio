Who else is building this

We are not first to autonomous cloud labs, and a page that pretended otherwise would not survive thirty seconds of scrutiny from anyone serious. Here is the actual competitive picture and where our advantage is real.

Capital is now treating autonomous laboratories as an infrastructure asset class, in the same conversation as chip fabs and data centres. That is good news for us: the category question is settled, and the remaining question is who serves which customer at what price.

Direct comparison

Where each player actually sits

PlayerWhat they offerOur read
Ginkgo Cloud Lab Launched March 2026. Browser access to an autonomous fleet of 70+ instruments on reconfigurable automation carts, with an AI agent that turns a plain-language protocol into a feasibility report and price. Open to academia through large biopharma. The closest competitor by a wide margin, and the strongest validation that this model is correct. They carry a Boston cost base, price is undisclosed and anchored on biopharma contracts, and they have no clinical substrate.
Emerald Cloud Lab The original cloud lab. 200+ instrument models under one software interface, a symbolic lab language, running 24/365. Technically deep and genuinely proven. Our position

The three things we can defend

Cost structure

Ginkgo launched US-based ADME services explicitly to match or beat Chinese vendor pricing — a defensive move that tells you price is the battleground. An India-based fleet attacks that structurally rather than promotionally.

The underserved buyer

Academic labs generate an enormous share of AI-designed candidates and cannot transact at biopharma rates. Serving them well is a volume wedge and a durable adoption motion as those scientists move into industry.

Clinical substrate

Apollo's pan-India EHR and biobank cannot be replicated by a Boston facility on any timeline that matters. Cost advantages get competed away; this one compounds.

What we do not claim. We are not first, not the largest, and not the most automated. We are betting that the winning position in validation infrastructure is the one that is affordable to the people generating the most hypotheses, and grounded in a patient population the incumbents cannot reach.
